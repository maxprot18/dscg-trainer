# Strategy and business models

**References:** Porter, Competitive Strategy (1980) and Competitive Advantage (1985) ; Ansoff (1957) ; Boston Consulting Group growth-share matrix ; Osterwalder and Pigneur, Business Model Generation (2010)

- **External analysis**: PESTEL (political, economic, social, technological, environmental, legal); **Porter's five forces**: rivalry among existing competitors, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, bargaining power of buyers, bargaining power of suppliers.
- **Internal analysis**: resources and **core competences**, value chain (primary and support activities), SWOT (strengths and weaknesses are internal, opportunities and threats are external).
- **Generic strategies (Porter)**: cost leadership, differentiation, focus (cost focus or differentiation focus on a niche). Being "stuck in the middle" is the classic trap.
- **Ansoff matrix**: market penetration (existing products, existing markets), market development (existing products, new markets), product development (new products, existing markets), diversification (new products, new markets: the riskiest option).
- **BCG matrix**: market growth rate vs **relative market share** (own share ÷ share of the largest competitor). Stars (high growth, high share), cash cows (low growth, high share: generate cash), question marks (high growth, low share: need cash), dogs (low growth, low share).
- **Growth modes**: organic growth, mergers and acquisitions, strategic alliances and joint ventures; vertical integration (upstream or downstream) vs horizontal integration; outsourcing.
- **Business model canvas** (9 building blocks): customer segments, value proposition, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure.
- **Business model types**: subscription, freemium (free basic version, paid premium), platform / two-sided marketplace with **network effects**, razor-and-blades, pay-per-use.

**Key formula:** relative market share = own market share ÷ market share of the largest competitor (above 1 = market leader)

## Key points
- Relative market share uses the largest competitor, not the market total.
- Diversification is the riskiest Ansoff option because both the product and the market are new.
- Network effects: the service becomes more valuable as more users join.
