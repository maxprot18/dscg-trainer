# Chairman’s statement and management commentary

**References:** IFRS Practice Statement 1 Management Commentary (IASB, non-mandatory, revised June 2025) ; SEC Regulation S-K, Item 303 (MD&A) ; ESMA Guidelines on Alternative Performance Measures (ESMA/2015/1415) ; IFRS 8 Operating Segments (règlement UE 2023/1803)

**Key issue:** the narrative part of an annual report is written by management and is not audited; the exam asks you to decode its vocabulary (basis points, organic growth, headwinds) and to recompute an adjusted figure from the reconciliation.

- **Chairman's statement** (letter to shareholders): a short, upbeat overview of the year, the dividend, board changes and the outlook. **Chief executive's review**: strategy and operational performance by business. **Financial review** (CFO): revenue, margins, cash flow, net debt, capital allocation, tax rate.
- **Management commentary / MD&A**: explains the figures "through the eyes of management". The revised IFRS Practice Statement 1 (June 2025) lists six areas of content: business model, strategy, resources and relationships, risks, external environment, and financial performance and financial position. SEC Item 303 requires results of operations, liquidity and capital resources, known trends and uncertainties, critical accounting estimates.
- **Alternative performance measures (APMs)**: measures not defined by IFRS (adjusted EBITDA, underlying profit, organic growth, free cash flow, net debt). ESMA guidelines: define each APM, give its **reconciliation** to the most directly reconcilable line item of the financial statements, explain why it is relevant, present comparatives, use it consistently over time, and do not display it with more prominence than IFRS measures.
- **Organic (like-for-like) growth**: growth excluding the effects of acquisitions, disposals and exchange rates (**constant currency**): acquisitions are removed from the current year, disposals from the prior-year base, and the currency effect is neutralised.
- **Segment reporting (IFRS 8)**: operating segments are those reviewed by the **chief operating decision maker** (CODM); a segment is reportable if its revenue, profit or loss, or assets reach 10% of the combined total; reportable segments must cover at least 75% of external revenue.
- **Vocabulary**: headwinds (unfavourable factors) vs tailwinds, year-on-year (YoY), top line (revenue) vs bottom line (net profit), one-off or exceptional items, guidance (targets announced to the market), run-rate, margin expansion/erosion, basis points (bp: 1 bp = 0.01 percentage point, 100 bp = 1 point).
- **Forward-looking statements**: comments on future performance, with a cautionary statement (safe harbour) that actual results may differ materially.

**Key formula:** organic growth = (current revenue − acquisitions' contribution − favourable FX effect) ÷ (prior-year revenue − revenue of disposed businesses) − 1

## Example
Financial review of Ashdown Group plc: "Revenue was €1,840m (2024: €1,600m), up 15%. 2025 includes €120m from Varela SA, acquired on 1 March 2025, and a favourable currency effect of €40m. 2024 included €100m from the logistics business sold on 31 December 2024."
- Reported growth = 1,840 ÷ 1,600 − 1 = 15%.
- Organic base 2024 = 1,600 − 100 = 1,500 ; organic revenue 2025 = 1,840 − 120 − 40 = 1,680.
- Organic growth = 1,680 ÷ 1,500 − 1 = 12%: the three-point gap with reported growth is a net effect, since the acquisition and the currency raise reported growth while the disposal lowers it.
- Elsewhere: "operating margin expanded by 80 bp to 12.3%" means a prior-year margin of 12.3 − 0.8 = 11.5%.

## Common mistakes
- Reading "+80 basis points" as +80% or as +8 points: 80 bp = 0.8 percentage point.
- Removing the disposed business from the current year instead of the prior year: by definition it no longer contributes in the current year; it is the prior-year base that must be adjusted.
- Taking "adjusted" or "underlying" profit as an IFRS figure: it is an APM chosen by management and must be reconciled to operating profit or profit for the year.
- Confusing top line and bottom line: the top line is revenue, the bottom line is profit for the year.

## Key points
- "Adjusted" or "underlying" figures are management's choices: always look for the reconciliation.
- Organic growth removes disposals from the prior-year base and acquisitions and FX from the current year.
- An improvement of 50 bp means +0.5 percentage point, not +50%.
