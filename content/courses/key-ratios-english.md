# Key financial ratios and performance indicators in English

**References:** IAS 33 §10 and §19-20 (earnings per share) ; IAS 7 (cash flows) ; IFRS 18 §53(a) and §69-71 (investing category, operating profit, profit before financing and income taxes) ; ESMA Guidelines on APMs (ESMA/2015/1415) — règlement UE 2023/1803 for IFRS (IFRS 18 inserted by Regulation (EU) 2026/338)

**Key issue:** the financial review and the broker notes express performance through ratios whose English names and definitions you must know; the exam asks you to recompute one of them from an extract and to check the company's own definition.

- **Profitability**: gross margin = gross profit ÷ revenue ; operating margin = operating profit ÷ revenue (often called EBIT; under IFRS 18, the investing category, e.g. the share of profit of associates, is outside operating profit but inside profit before financing and income taxes) ; EBITDA margin = EBITDA ÷ revenue ; **ROCE** (return on capital employed) = operating profit ÷ capital employed ; ROE (return on equity) = profit attributable to shareholders ÷ equity.
- **Capital employed**: usually total equity + non-current liabilities (equivalently total assets − current liabilities), or equity + net debt; often averaged between opening and closing balance sheets. ROCE is compared with the weighted average cost of capital (WACC) to assess value creation.
- **Per share data**: **basic EPS** (*résultat par action*) = profit attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent ÷ **weighted average** number of ordinary shares outstanding during the period (IAS 33 §10, §19-20; treasury shares are excluded); diluted EPS adds potential ordinary shares (options, convertibles). P/E ratio = share price ÷ EPS ; dividend per share (DPS), dividend cover = EPS ÷ DPS, payout ratio = DPS ÷ EPS, dividend yield = DPS ÷ share price.
- **Financial structure**: net debt = borrowings (+ lease liabilities, according to the company's definition) − cash and cash equivalents ; gearing = net debt ÷ equity ; leverage = net debt ÷ EBITDA (often capped at 3× in loan **covenants**) ; interest cover = operating profit ÷ net finance costs.
- **Liquidity and working capital**: current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities ; quick ratio excludes inventories ; DSO (debtor days) = trade receivables ÷ revenue × 365 ; DPO (creditor days), DIO (inventory days) ; cash conversion cycle = DIO + DSO − DPO.
- **Cash**: **free cash flow** (FCF) has no single definition; a common one is net cash from operating activities − capital expenditure (capex) + disposal proceeds, sometimes also less lease repayments and interest. Cash conversion = FCF (or operating cash flow) ÷ profit or EBITDA.

**Key formulas:** EPS = attributable profit ÷ weighted average shares ; weighted shares = opening shares + new shares × months outstanding ÷ 12 − shares bought back × months since buyback ÷ 12 ; gearing = net debt ÷ equity

## Example
Extract from Wexford plc, 2025 (€m): revenue 3,000; operating profit 360; depreciation and amortisation 140; net finance costs 45; profit for the year 230, of which 20 attributable to non-controlling interests; equity 1,200; non-current liabilities 800; borrowings 900; lease liabilities 100; cash 250; current assets 900; current liabilities 600. 100 million shares at 1 January, 20 million issued at market price on 1 October 2025; share price €30.
- EBITDA = 360 + 140 = 500 ; EBITDA margin = 500 ÷ 3,000 = 16.7% ; operating margin = 12.0% ; ROCE = 360 ÷ (1,200 + 800) = 18.0%.
- Net debt = 900 + 100 − 250 = 750 ; gearing = 750 ÷ 1,200 = 62.5% ; leverage = 750 ÷ 500 = 1.5× ; interest cover = 360 ÷ 45 = 8× ; current ratio = 900 ÷ 600 = 1.5.
- Weighted shares = 100 + 20 × 3 ÷ 12 = 105 million ; basic EPS = (230 − 20) ÷ 105 = €2.00 ; P/E = 30 ÷ 2.00 = 15.

## Common mistakes
- Dividing by the year-end number of shares (120 million) or using total profit (230) for EPS: IAS 33 requires the weighted average (105) and the profit attributable to the parent (210).
- Computing ROCE on reported operating profit when the company defines it on adjusted operating profit: apply the company's definition, then state which one you used.
- Subtracting all investing cash flows to obtain FCF, including acquisitions of subsidiaries: most definitions deduct capex only, and the ESMA guidelines require the company's definition to be disclosed and applied consistently.
- Comparing gearing ratios without checking the denominator: net debt ÷ equity and net debt ÷ (net debt + equity) give different figures (62.5% vs 38.5% in the example).

## Key points
- EPS uses the weighted average, not the year-end number of shares, and profit attributable to the parent (after non-controlling interests).
- Always apply the company's own definition of an APM (FCF, net debt, adjusted EBIT).
- Leverage (net debt ÷ EBITDA) and interest cover are the ratios watched in covenants; gearing is sometimes defined as net debt ÷ (net debt + equity).
