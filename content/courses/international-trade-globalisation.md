# International trade and globalisation

**References:** GATT 1994, art. I (most-favoured-nation treatment) and art. III (national treatment) — WTO agreements ; TFEU art. 206-207 (common commercial policy) ; IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) ; Regulation (EU) 2023/956 (CBAM)

- **Comparative advantage** (Ricardo): a country gains from specialising in goods it produces at the lowest **opportunity cost**, even if it has no **absolute advantage** in any good.
- **Trade barriers**: tariffs (customs duties), quotas, non-tariff barriers (standards, licences), export subsidies. **Anti-dumping duties** target imports sold below normal value. Tariffs raise domestic prices and invite retaliation (trade wars).
- **WTO principles**: most-favoured-nation (no discrimination between trading partners) and national treatment (no discrimination between imported and domestic goods once on the market).
- **Levels of integration**: free trade area (no internal tariffs, each member keeps its own external tariff) → customs union (plus a common external tariff; the EU is one) → single market (free movement of goods, services, capital and people).
- **Balance of payments**: current account (goods, services, primary and secondary income), capital account, financial account. A **trade deficit** means imports of goods exceed exports.
- **Exchange rates**: an appreciation of the domestic currency makes exports dearer and imports cheaper. **Terms of trade** = export price index ÷ import price index × 100.
- **Globalisation trends**: global value chains, offshoring, reshoring / nearshoring, "friend-shoring" and de-risking of supply chains. The EU **carbon border adjustment mechanism** (CBAM) puts a carbon price on certain imports (e.g. steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers).

## Key points
- Free trade area ≠ customs union: only the latter has a common external tariff.
- Trade policy is an exclusive competence of the EU: member states do not negotiate trade agreements alone.
- A current account deficit is not necessarily "bad": it is financed by capital inflows (financial account).
