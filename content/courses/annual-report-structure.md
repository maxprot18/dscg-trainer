# Structure of an annual report

**References:** UK Companies Act 2006, s.414A-414D (strategic report), s.415 (directors' report) and s.420 (directors' remuneration report) ; SEC Form 10-K ; Directive 2013/34/EU ; Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/815 (ESEF) ; Code de commerce, art. L. 232-1 (rapport de gestion)

**Key issue:** an annual report is several hundred pages long; the exam gives extracts and asks where a piece of information is found, who is responsible for it and whether it is audited.

**UK annual report (listed company)**, in three blocks:
- **Strategic report** (s.414A-414D): chair's statement, chief executive's review, business model, strategy, key performance indicators (KPIs), **principal risks and uncertainties**, section 172 statement (how the directors have had regard to employees, customers, suppliers and the community), sustainability and climate information. It is narrative and forward-looking.
- **Governance**: board of directors, corporate governance report (compliance with the UK Corporate Governance Code), committee reports (audit, nomination), **directors' remuneration report** (remuneration policy, submitted to a binding shareholder vote, and the annual report on pay, advisory vote), directors' report (dividend recommended, share capital, political donations, going concern statement).
- **Financial statements**: independent auditor's report, consolidated income statement, statement of comprehensive income, balance sheet, statement of changes in equity, cash flow statement and notes, then the parent company's own accounts.

**US Form 10-K** (filed with the SEC within 60, 75 or 90 days of year end, depending on the filer's size): Item 1 Business, Item 1A Risk Factors, Item 7 Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A), Item 8 Financial Statements and Supplementary Data, Item 9A Controls and Procedures (internal control over financial reporting, Sarbanes-Oxley Act s.404).

**France**: the *rapport financier annuel* (often included in a *document d'enregistrement universel*, universal registration document, URD), with the *rapport de gestion* (management report), the *rapport sur le gouvernement d'entreprise* and, under the CSRD, the sustainability statement (*état de durabilité*) in a dedicated section of the management report.

**ESEF**: EU issuers prepare their annual financial report in XHTML, with the IFRS consolidated statements tagged in iXBRL so that the figures are machine-readable.

**Vocabulary**: group (consolidated) vs company (parent-only) accounts, financial year (*exercice*), "year ended 31 December 2025", comparatives (*chiffres comparatifs*), restated (*retraité*), notes to the financial statements (*annexe*), glossary of APMs at the back of the report.

## Example
An analyst reads the 2025 annual report of Northbrook plc and needs three items. (1) The group's main risks: strategic report, section "principal risks and uncertainties". (2) The chief executive's bonus: directors' remuneration report, single total figure table. (3) The dividend paid and its effect on equity: statement of changes in equity and the dividends note.
- The dividends note shows: retained earnings at 1 January €500m, profit for the year €80m, dividends paid €50m. Closing retained earnings = 500 + 80 − 50 = €530m.
- Only item (3) is in the audited financial statements; items (1) and (2) are "other information" that the auditor reads for consistency (and, in the UK, the auditable part of the remuneration report is audited).

## Common mistakes
- Looking for management's explanation of a margin fall in Item 1A (risk factors) or Item 8 (financial statements) of a 10-K: the discussion of results is in Item 7, the MD&A.
- Treating dividends paid as an expense of the income statement: dividends are a distribution to owners, shown in the statement of changes in equity, so they reduce retained earnings without affecting profit.
- Believing the whole annual report is audited: the auditor's opinion covers the financial statements; the front half is other information.
- Reading "company accounts" as the group's figures: company accounts are the parent on its own; the group figures are the consolidated accounts.

## Key points
- Three blocks in a UK report: strategic report, governance (including remuneration), financial statements.
- The audited part is the financial statements; the narrative front section is other information that the auditor reads but does not audit (UK auditors also state whether the strategic report and directors' report are consistent with the financial statements).
- In a 10-K, the MD&A is Item 7 and the risk factors are Item 1A.
