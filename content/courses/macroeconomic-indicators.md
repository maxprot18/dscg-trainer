# Macroeconomic indicators: growth, inflation, unemployment

**References:** European System of Accounts ESA 2010 (Regulation (EU) No 549/2013) ; ILO definition of unemployment ; Eurostat HICP methodology

- **GDP** (gross domestic product, *PIB*): market value of all final goods and services produced within a country over a period. Three approaches: output (sum of value added), expenditure (C + I + G + X − M) and income.
- **Nominal GDP** is measured at current prices; **real GDP** at constant prices. The **GDP deflator** (nominal ÷ real × 100) measures the price change of everything produced.
- **Recession**: a widely used rule of thumb is two consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth; some bodies (e.g. the NBER in the US) use broader criteria.
- **Inflation**: sustained rise in the general price level, usually measured by a consumer price index (CPI; in the euro area the HICP, *IPCH*). **Core inflation** excludes volatile items such as energy and food.
- **Disinflation** = inflation is falling but still positive; **deflation** = prices are falling (negative inflation); **stagflation** = high inflation combined with stagnant growth and rising unemployment.
- **Unemployment rate** (ILO): unemployed ÷ labour force. An unemployed person has no work, is available and is actively looking for a job. The labour force (*population active*) = employed + unemployed.
- **Output gap**: actual GDP minus potential GDP; a negative gap signals spare capacity.

**Key formulas:** real growth = (1 + nominal growth) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1 ≈ nominal growth − inflation ; unemployment rate = unemployed ÷ (employed + unemployed)

## Key points
- Disinflation is not deflation: prices still rise, only more slowly.
- The unemployment rate is calculated on the labour force, not on the total working-age population.
- The approximation "nominal − inflation" becomes inaccurate when rates are high: use the exact formula.
- GDP measures production on the national territory; GNI (*RNB*) adds net income received from abroad.
