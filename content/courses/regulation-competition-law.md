# Regulation, competition and compliance

**References:** TFEU art. 101 (anti-competitive agreements), 102 (abuse of a dominant position) and 107 (State aid) ; Regulation (EC) No 1/2003, art. 23 (fines) ; Council Regulation (EC) No 139/2004 (EU Merger Regulation) ; Regulation (EU) 2022/1925 (Digital Markets Act) ; Directive (EU) 2019/1937 (whistleblowers)

- **Cartels** (art. 101): agreements or concerted practices between competitors, such as price-fixing, market sharing or bid rigging, are prohibited. A **leniency programme** grants immunity or a reduction of fines to firms that report a cartel and cooperate.
- **Abuse of dominance** (art. 102): holding a dominant position is lawful; abusing it is not (predatory pricing, refusal to supply, tying, exclusive dealing, excessive pricing).
- **Fines** can reach 10% of the undertaking's total worldwide turnover in the preceding business year.
- **Merger control**: concentrations with an EU dimension must be notified to the European Commission ("one-stop shop") and cannot be implemented before clearance (**standstill obligation**; breaching it is "gun-jumping"). EU dimension (main test): combined worldwide turnover above €5,000m and EU-wide turnover of each of at least two parties above €250m, unless each achieves more than two-thirds of its EU turnover in one and the same member state. Substantive test: significant impediment to effective competition (SIEC).
- **Remedies**: structural (divestiture of a business) or behavioural (access commitments).
- **Market concentration**: the HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index) is the sum of squared market shares. The Commission is unlikely to find horizontal concerns when the post-merger HHI is above 2,000 and the increase (delta) is below 150, absent special circumstances.
- **Ex ante regulation**: the DMA imposes obligations on designated "gatekeepers" of digital platforms.
- **Compliance**: compliance programmes, codes of conduct, whistleblowing channels, anti-bribery laws (French Sapin II law, UK Bribery Act, US FCPA), anti-money laundering (AML) and sanctions screening. National authorities: Autorité de la concurrence (FR), CMA (UK), FTC and DoJ (US antitrust).

**Key formulas:** HHI = Σ sᵢ² (shares in %) ; delta HHI of a two-firm merger = 2 × s₁ × s₂

## Key points
- Dominance is not illegal; its abuse is.
- Turnover thresholds decide which authority reviews a deal, not whether it is harmful.
- "Antitrust" (US) = competition law (EU/UK).
