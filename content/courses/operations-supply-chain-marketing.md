# Operations, supply chain and marketing

**References:** Ohno, Toyota Production System (1988) ; McCarthy (1960) and Booms and Bitner (1981) on the marketing mix ; Kotler, Marketing Management

- **Supply chain** (*chaîne logistique*): procurement/purchasing (*achats*), production, warehousing, distribution, reverse logistics (returns, recycling). Upstream = suppliers, downstream = customers.
- **Operations vocabulary**: lead time (*délai d'approvisionnement*), throughput, capacity utilisation, bottleneck (*goulet d'étranglement*), safety stock (*stock de sécurité*), reorder point, stock-out (*rupture de stock*), backlog (orders not yet fulfilled).
- **Lean management**: eliminate waste (*gaspillage*), **just-in-time** (JIT: produce or receive only what is needed, when needed, which lowers inventories but increases exposure to disruptions), kaizen (continuous improvement), total quality management.
- **Resilience**: nearshoring/reshoring, dual sourcing, buffer stocks; supply chain disruption.
- **Marketing mix**: 4Ps (product, price, place, promotion), extended to 7Ps for services (people, process, physical evidence).
- **STP**: segmentation (dividing the market), targeting (choosing segments), positioning (the place the brand occupies in customers' minds); unique selling proposition (USP), brand equity.
- **Pricing**: penetration pricing (low price to gain share quickly) vs price skimming (high launch price, lowered later); premium pricing, dynamic pricing.
- **Customer metrics**: customer acquisition cost (CAC), customer lifetime value (CLV), churn rate (share of customers lost over a period), conversion rate, net promoter score (NPS).

**Key formulas:** inventory turnover = cost of sales ÷ average inventory ; days inventory outstanding (DIO) = average inventory ÷ cost of sales × 365 ; churn rate = customers lost ÷ customers at the start of the period

## Key points
- "Place" in the 4Ps means distribution, not the shop's location only.
- Inventory ratios use cost of sales (not revenue) when inventories are valued at cost.
- JIT reduces holding costs but makes the firm vulnerable to supplier failures.
