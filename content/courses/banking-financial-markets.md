# Banking and financial markets

**References:** Basel III framework (BCBS), implemented in the EU by Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 (CRR) and Directive 2013/36/EU (CRD) ; Directive 2014/65/EU (MiFID II) ; Regulation (EU) No 596/2014 (Market Abuse Regulation)

- **Banks**: commercial (retail) banks take deposits and grant loans, earning a **net interest margin**; investment banks advise on M&A, underwrite securities issues and trade on markets.
- **Prudential regulation (Basel III)**: banks hold capital against risk-weighted assets (RWA). **CET1 ratio** = Common Equity Tier 1 capital ÷ RWA; minimum 4.5%, plus buffers such as the 2.5% capital conservation buffer. Liquidity ratios: **LCR** (30-day stress) and **NSFR** (stable funding), each at least 100%.
- **Supervision**: in the euro area, the ECB directly supervises significant banks (Single Supervisory Mechanism); other authorities include the EBA, ESMA, the ACPR and AMF (France), the PRA and FCA (UK), the Fed and SEC (US).
- **Markets**: money market (short-term funding) vs capital markets (equity and debt). **Primary market** = issue of new securities (e.g. an IPO, *introduction en bourse*); **secondary market** = trading of existing securities.
- **Bonds**: face (par) value, coupon, maturity, yield. Bond prices and yields move in **opposite directions**: when market rates rise, existing bond prices fall. Credit ratings: **investment grade** (BBB−/Baa3 or above) vs **high yield** ("junk").
- **Yield curve**: yields by maturity; an **inverted** curve (short rates above long rates) is often read as a recession signal.
- **Derivatives**: futures, forwards, options and swaps, used for **hedging** (*couverture*) or speculation.
- **Market abuse**: insider dealing (trading on inside information) and market manipulation are prohibited.

**Key formulas:** current yield = annual coupon ÷ market price ; one-year yield to maturity = (coupon + face value) ÷ price − 1

## Key points
- A bond trading below par yields more than its coupon rate.
- The CET1 ratio is calculated on risk-weighted assets, not on total assets (the leverage ratio uses total exposure).
- "Share" (UK) = "stock" (US); "shares" ≠ "bonds" (debt securities, *obligations*).
