# Mergers, acquisitions and corporate finance

**References:** Directive 2004/25/EC (takeover bids) ; IFRS 3 Business Combinations (règlement UE 2023/1803) ; Council Regulation (EC) No 139/2004 (EU Merger Regulation)

- **Merger** (two companies combine into one) vs **acquisition / takeover** (one company buys control of another, the **target**). Friendly deals are recommended by the target's board; **hostile** bids are not.
- **Types**: horizontal (competitors), vertical (supplier or customer), conglomerate (unrelated business).
- **Process**: screening, non-disclosure agreement (NDA), letter of intent or term sheet, **due diligence** (financial, legal, tax, ESG), share purchase agreement (SPA), signing, regulatory clearances, closing, post-merger integration.
- **Public offers**: a **tender offer** is made directly to shareholders; crossing a control threshold triggers a **mandatory bid** (30% of voting rights in France and the UK). A **squeeze-out** lets a bidder holding a very large majority buy out the remaining minority.
- **Consideration**: cash offer, share-for-share exchange, or a mix; an **earn-out** makes part of the price contingent on future performance. In a **leveraged buyout** (LBO) a private equity fund finances the deal largely with debt.
- **Valuation vocabulary**: equity value (market capitalisation), **enterprise value** (EV = equity value + net debt), multiples (EV/EBITDA, P/E ratio), **control premium** over the undisturbed share price, cost and revenue **synergies**, **goodwill** (excess of consideration over net identifiable assets, IFRS 3).
- **Takeover defences**: poison pill, white knight, sale of the "crown jewels".
- **Corporate finance**: dividend payout ratio, share buyback (*rachat d'actions*), rights issue (*augmentation de capital avec DPS*), spin-off, divestment (*cession*).

**Key formulas:** premium = offer price ÷ undisturbed price − 1 ; EV = equity value + net debt ; payout ratio = dividends ÷ net income

## Key points
- Enterprise value is the value of the whole business (for all capital providers); equity value is what shareholders receive.
- Many announced synergies are never delivered: integration risk is the main cause of value destruction.
- "Merger" in everyday English often describes what is legally an acquisition.
