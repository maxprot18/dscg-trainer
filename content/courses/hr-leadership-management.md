# Human resources, leadership and management

**References:** Maslow, "A Theory of Human Motivation" (1943) ; Herzberg, The Motivation to Work (1959) ; McGregor, The Human Side of Enterprise (1960) ; Burns (1978) and Bass (1985) on transformational leadership ; UK Employment Rights Act 1996 (redundancy, dismissal)

**Key issue:** HR texts in UE 6 (restructuring announcements, engagement surveys, leadership profiles) test precise vocabulary — redundancy vs dismissal — and the ability to apply a motivation or leadership model to a short case and to compute HR indicators.

- **Employee life cycle**: recruitment (job posting, shortlisting, interview), hiring, **onboarding** (*intégration*), training, performance **appraisal** or review (*entretien annuel d'évaluation*), promotion, departure (exit interview).
- **Leaving a company**: to resign or quit (*démissionner*), to be dismissed or fired (*licencié pour motif personnel*: conduct or performance), to be made **redundant** / laid off (*licencié pour motif économique*: the job itself disappears, e.g. a site closure, usually with a severance payment), to retire (*partir à la retraite*), notice period (*préavis*), gardening leave.
- **Skills**: upskilling (improving skills for the current job) vs reskilling (learning skills for a different job); talent management, succession planning for key positions, skills gap.
- **Pay and benefits**: salary (monthly/annual), wages (hourly), bonus, fringe benefits (*avantages en nature*), profit-sharing, pension contributions; the total is the compensation package.
- **Motivation theories**: Maslow's hierarchy of needs (physiological, safety, belonging, esteem, self-actualisation: a lower need must be met before the next one motivates); Herzberg's two-factor theory: **hygiene factors** (pay, working conditions, supervision) prevent dissatisfaction, **motivators** (recognition, responsibility, achievement, growth) create satisfaction; McGregor's Theory X (people avoid work, need control) vs Theory Y (people seek responsibility and can be trusted).
- **Leadership styles**: autocratic, democratic (participative), laissez-faire; **transactional** (rewards and sanctions for meeting targets) vs **transformational** (vision, inspiration, individual development of followers). Management administers systems; leadership gives direction and meaning.
- **Workplace trends**: hybrid and remote work, employee engagement, well-being and burnout prevention, diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI), employer branding, quiet quitting.

**Key formulas:** staff turnover rate = number of leavers ÷ average headcount × 100 ; average headcount = (opening + closing) ÷ 2 ; absenteeism rate = days lost ÷ days that should have been worked × 100

## Example
A shared service centre starts the year with 230 employees and ends it with 250; 36 people left during the year (resignations and dismissals); 1,200 working days were lost to sickness over 220 working days per person.
Turnover = 36 ÷ ((230 + 250) ÷ 2) = 36 ÷ 240 = **15%** (not 36 ÷ 250 = 14.4%). Absenteeism = 1,200 ÷ (240 × 220) = 1,200 ÷ 52,800 = **2.27%**. The engagement survey shows demotivation despite above-market pay and modern offices: under Herzberg, hygiene factors are already satisfied; the manager should act on motivators — ownership of whole processes, recognition, development — rather than on another pay rise or closer supervision.

## Common mistakes
- Calling employees whose warehouse closes "dismissed for misconduct": their jobs disappear, they are made redundant.
- Expecting a further pay rise or better break areas to motivate a team already well paid: for Herzberg these are hygiene factors.
- Increasing supervision to raise motivation: this is Theory X and tends to reduce autonomy and engagement.
- Dividing leavers by the closing headcount instead of the average headcount.

## Key points
- "Redundancy" concerns the position, "dismissal" in everyday usage the person's conduct or performance; in UK law, redundancy is itself one of the potentially fair reasons for dismissal (Employment Rights Act 1996, s.98(2)(c)).
- In Herzberg's model, a pay rise removes dissatisfaction but does not, on its own, motivate durably.
- Staff turnover is computed on the average headcount, not on the closing headcount.
