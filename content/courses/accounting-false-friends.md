# False friends and translation pitfalls in accounting and finance

**References:** IAS 2 §9 and §28 ; IAS 36 §6 ; IAS 37 §10 ; IFRS 9 appendix A ("loss allowance") ; IAS 1 §7 (règlement UE 2023/1803) ; usual UK/US professional terminology

**Key issue:** a false friend is a word that looks the same in French and English but means something different; in the exam, translation questions (press releases, e-mails to a head office, extracts of accounts) are built around these words, and a literal translation is always one of the distractors.

**Provision ≠ provision**
- In IFRS English, a provision is a liability of uncertain timing or amount (IAS 37 §10): *provision pour litiges* → provision for legal claims (correct, because it is a liability).
- A French *dépréciation* (or old-style *provision pour dépréciation*) of an asset is an impairment loss; on inventories, a write-down to net realisable value (IAS 2 §28); on receivables, IFRS 9 speaks of a loss allowance. Never "depreciation of receivables".
- A "reserve" is a component of equity (share premium, retained earnings), never a liability.

**Usual pitfalls** (French → correct English, *wrong literal translation*)
- *résultat* → profit or loss, net income (*result*) ; *résultat d'exploitation* → operating profit (*exploitation result*) ; *chiffre d'affaires* → revenue, turnover (*business figure*)
- *exercice* → financial year, FY (*exercise*: a drill or a share option exercise) ; *bilan* → balance sheet (*bilan*) ; *actuel* → current (*actual* means real, as in "actual vs budget")
- *charges* → expenses (in English, charges are fees or levies; "a charge" in a P&L is a one-off expense such as an impairment charge) ; *produits* → income, revenue (*products* are goods)
- *bénéfice* → profit (*benefit* is an advantage or an employee benefit, *avantage du personnel*) ; *actif / passif* → assets / equity and liabilities ; *capitaux propres* → equity
- *commissaire aux comptes* → statutory auditor (*commissioner*) ; *expert-comptable* → chartered accountant ; *librairie* → bookshop (*library* = *bibliothèque*)
- *amortissement* → depreciation or amortisation ; *dépréciation* → impairment ; *plus-value de cession* → gain on disposal ; *stock* → inventories (in US English, stock means shares)
- *actions / obligations* → shares / bonds (*obligations* means commitments) ; *trésorerie* → cash (treasury is the department) ; *société* → company (*society*)

## Example
Translate for the UK head office: « Sur l'exercice 2025, la société a constaté une provision pour litige de 120 k€, une dépréciation des stocks de 30 k€ et une plus-value de cession de 45 k€. Le résultat d'exploitation actuel est de 610 k€. »
- "In financial year 2025, the company recognised a provision for a legal claim of €120k, an inventory write-down of €30k and a gain on disposal of €45k. Current operating profit is €610k."
- Check: the provision is a liability (IAS 37), so "provision" is right; the inventory item is an asset loss in value, so "write-down" or "impairment", not "provision"; *actuel* is "current", not "actual".
- Net effect on operating profit of the three items: −120 − 30 + 45 = −€105k.

## Common mistakes
- Writing "we have booked a provision for doubtful debts": under IFRS 9 the correct wording is a loss allowance (impairment) on trade receivables; "reserve" is wrong too, since it designates equity.
- Translating *dépréciation de l'écart d'acquisition* by "goodwill amortisation" or "depreciation of the acquisition gap": goodwill is not amortised under IFRS, it is tested for impairment, hence "goodwill impairment charge".
- Using "the exercise" for *l'exercice*, "benefit" for *bénéfice* or "actually" for *actuellement*: write the financial year, profit and currently.

## Key points
- Translate *provision* by "provision" only when it is a liability; otherwise use impairment, write-down or (loss) allowance.
- "Résultat" is never "result" in a set of accounts; "depreciation" is a systematic allocation (*amortissement*), not a loss in value.
- *Éventuellement* means possibly, *actuellement* currently: avoid "eventually" and "actually".
