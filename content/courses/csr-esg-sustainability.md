# CSR, ESG and sustainability

**References:** Directive (EU) 2022/2464 (CSRD) and ESRS (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772) ; Directive (EU) 2025/794 ("stop-the-clock") ; Regulation (EU) 2020/852 (EU Taxonomy) ; Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 (SFDR) ; GHG Protocol Corporate Standard

- **CSR** (corporate social responsibility, *RSE*): voluntary integration of social and environmental concerns in business operations and stakeholder relations. **ESG** (environmental, social, governance) is the investor-side framework used to assess non-financial risks and performance.
- **GHG emissions** (GHG Protocol): **Scope 1** = direct emissions from owned or controlled sources; **Scope 2** = indirect emissions from purchased electricity, heat or steam (location-based or market-based); **Scope 3** = all other indirect emissions in the value chain (suppliers, use of sold products, business travel…), often the largest share.
- **Double materiality** (CSRD/ESRS): impact materiality (the company's impacts on people and the environment) and financial materiality (sustainability matters that create risks or opportunities for the company). A matter is material if it meets either test.
- **CSRD**: sustainability statement in a dedicated section of the management report, prepared under ESRS and subject to limited assurance. Phase-in dates were postponed in 2025 (Directive (EU) 2025/794) and the "Omnibus I" simplification agreed at the end of 2025 narrows the scope to the largest companies: always check the texts in force.
- **EU Taxonomy**: classification of environmentally sustainable activities — substantial contribution to one of six environmental objectives, "do no significant harm" (DNSH) to the others, minimum safeguards. Companies disclose taxonomy-aligned turnover, CapEx and OpEx.
- **SFDR**: disclosure rules for financial products ("Article 8" products promote environmental or social characteristics; "Article 9" products have a sustainable investment objective).
- **Greenwashing**: misleading claims about environmental performance. **Net zero**: balancing residual emissions with removals, after deep cuts.

**Key formula:** carbon intensity = emissions (tCO₂e) ÷ revenue (€m)

## Key points
- Scope 2 covers purchased energy; emissions from suppliers' production fall in Scope 3.
- Double materiality: either the impact view or the financial view is enough to make a topic material.
- An absolute emissions cut and an intensity cut are different indicators: intensity can fall while emissions rise.
