# CSR, ESG and sustainability

**References:** Directive (EU) 2022/2464 (CSRD) and ESRS (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772) ; Directive (EU) 2025/794 ("stop-the-clock") ; Directive (EU) 2026/470 ("Omnibus I") ; Regulation (EU) 2020/852 (EU Taxonomy) ; Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 (SFDR) ; GHG Protocol Corporate Standard

**Key issue:** sustainability statements and ESG press coverage are now standard UE 6 reading material; you must classify emissions by scope, explain double materiality and read an emissions table critically (absolute vs intensity).

- **CSR** (corporate social responsibility, *RSE*): voluntary integration of social and environmental concerns in business operations and stakeholder relations. **ESG** (environmental, social, governance) is the investor-side framework used to assess non-financial risks and performance, often through ratings.
- **GHG emissions** (GHG Protocol): **Scope 1** = direct emissions from owned or controlled sources (plants, company vehicles); **Scope 2** = indirect emissions from purchased electricity, heat or steam (location-based or market-based method); **Scope 3** = all other indirect emissions in the value chain — purchased goods (upstream), use and end of life of sold products (downstream), business travel — often the largest share. The emissions of a car maker's vehicles while customers drive them are downstream Scope 3; they are not left out of the inventory.
- **Double materiality** (CSRD/ESRS): impact materiality (the company's impacts on people and the environment) and financial materiality (sustainability matters that create risks or opportunities for the company). A matter is material if it meets either test; the assessment determines which ESRS disclosures are reported.
- **CSRD**: sustainability statement in a dedicated section of the management report, prepared under ESRS and subject to limited assurance. Phase-in dates were postponed in 2025 (Directive (EU) 2025/794). The "Omnibus I" Directive (EU) 2026/470 (published in February 2026, to be transposed by 19 March 2027) limits the CSRD to companies with more than 1,000 employees and over €450m net turnover, removes listed SMEs from scope and keeps limited assurance only; it also narrows the due diligence directive (CSDDD) to companies with more than 5,000 employees and €1.5bn turnover. Check national transposition for the year studied.
- **EU Taxonomy**: classification of environmentally sustainable activities — substantial contribution to one of six environmental objectives, "do no significant harm" (DNSH) to the others, minimum safeguards. Companies disclose taxonomy-eligible and taxonomy-aligned turnover, CapEx and OpEx.
- **SFDR**: disclosure rules for financial products ("Article 8" products promote environmental or social characteristics; "Article 9" products have a sustainable investment objective).
- **Greenwashing**: misleading claims about environmental performance, now sanctioned as unfair commercial practices. **Net zero**: balancing residual emissions with removals, after deep cuts; offsets alone are not a net zero strategy.

**Key formulas:** total emissions = Scope 1 + Scope 2 + Scope 3 ; carbon intensity = emissions (tCO₂e) ÷ revenue (€m) ; change in intensity ≠ change in absolute emissions when revenue moves

## Example
Helix Foods reports (tCO₂e): year 1 — Scope 1 15,000, Scope 2 6,000, Scope 3 60,000, revenue €300m; year 2 — Scope 1 16,000, Scope 2 5,000, Scope 3 72,000, revenue €360m.
Total emissions: 81,000 then 93,000, up **14.8%**. Intensity: 81,000 ÷ 300 = 270 then 93,000 ÷ 360 = **258.3 tCO₂e per €m**, down 4.3%. The company may claim a lower intensity but not a reduction in emissions; Scope 3 represents 72,000 ÷ 93,000 = **77%** of the total, so supplier engagement matters more than the new electricity contract that cut Scope 2.

## Common mistakes
- Classifying the emissions of sold products in Scope 1 (because the company made them) or Scope 2 (because they involve energy): they are downstream Scope 3.
- Excluding emissions that occur after the sale: the GHG Protocol includes use and end of life of sold products.
- Presenting a fall in carbon intensity as a fall in emissions when revenue grew faster than emissions.
- Requiring both materiality tests to be met: double materiality means either one is enough.

## Key points
- Scope 2 covers purchased energy; emissions from suppliers' production fall in Scope 3.
- Double materiality: either the impact view or the financial view is enough to make a topic material.
- An absolute emissions cut and an intensity cut are different indicators: intensity can fall while emissions rise.
