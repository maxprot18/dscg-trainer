# IFRS and accounting terminology (UK and US usage)

**References:** IFRS 18 appendix A and §103 (formerly IAS 1 §7 and §54 ; Regulation (EU) 2026/338) ; IAS 16 §6 ; IAS 36 §6 and §18 ; IAS 37 §10 ; IAS 38 §8 ; IFRS 13 §9 (règlement UE 2023/1803)

**Key issue:** the same French word (*amortissement*, *stocks*, *provision*) has several English equivalents depending on the asset concerned and on whether the document is British, American or IFRS; the exam asks for the term consistent with the context of the extract.

**IFRS terms to know**
- Carrying amount (*valeur nette comptable*): amount at which an asset is recognised after deducting accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment losses (IAS 16 §6).
- Depreciation (PP&E, IAS 16) and amortisation (intangible assets, IAS 38) both translate *amortissement*: a systematic allocation of the depreciable amount over the useful life. A French *dépréciation* is an impairment loss (IAS 36 §6): the excess of carrying amount over recoverable amount.
- Recoverable amount: the **higher** of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use (IAS 36 §18). Fair value: the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date (IFRS 13 §9).
- Provision (IAS 37 §10): a liability of uncertain timing or amount; an accrual (*charge à payer*) is a liability whose amount is far more certain, shown in trade and other payables.
- Other comprehensive income (OCI, *autres éléments du résultat global*): items of income and expense not recognised in profit or loss (revaluation surplus, remeasurements of defined benefit plans, cash flow hedge reserve, translation differences). Non-controlling interests (*participations ne donnant pas le contrôle*), retained earnings (*réserves et report à nouveau*), goodwill (*écart d'acquisition*).

**UK vs US usage** (UK first, then US; IFRS wording in brackets)
- Turnover / sales (revenue) – *chiffre d'affaires* ; stock / inventory (inventories) – *stocks*
- Debtors / accounts receivable (trade receivables) – *créances clients* ; creditors / accounts payable (trade payables) – *dettes fournisseurs*
- Shares / stock (shares) – *actions* ; share premium / additional paid-in capital – *prime d'émission* ; profit and loss account / income statement – *compte de résultat*
- Fixed assets / long-lived assets (non-current assets) – *immobilisations* ; gearing / leverage – *endettement*

## Example
A machine has a carrying amount of €800k at 31 December 2025. Its fair value less costs of disposal is €650k and its value in use (discounted cash flows) is €720k.
- Recoverable amount = higher of 650 and 720 = €720k.
- Impairment loss = 800 − 720 = €80k, recognised in profit or loss (IAS 36 §59-60); in French, a *dépréciation* of 80 k€, not an *amortissement*.
- Had the figures been 650 and 850, no impairment would be recognised (carrying amount below recoverable amount).

## Common mistakes
- Defining the recoverable amount as the **lower** of the two values: IAS 36 takes the higher, because a rational owner keeps or sells the asset, whichever is worth more.
- Reading "turnover" as staff turnover or inventory turnover in a UK income statement: on the first line of the accounts, turnover means revenue.
- Translating *dépréciation* by "depreciation" or *prime d'émission* by "issue premium": a loss in value is an impairment, and the premium is share premium (UK) or additional paid-in capital (US).

## Key points
- "Stock" means inventories in traditional UK accounts but shares in US English ("common stock").
- US spelling is "amortization", IFRS (British spelling) uses "amortisation"; IFRS adopted in the EU use British wording ("inventories", "trade receivables").
- An asset is impaired when its carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount, the higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use.
