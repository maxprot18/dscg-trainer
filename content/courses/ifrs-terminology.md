# IFRS and accounting terminology (UK and US usage)

**References:** IAS 16 §6 ; IAS 38 §8 ; IAS 36 §6 and §18 ; IFRS 13 §9 ; IAS 1 §7 (règlement UE 2023/1803)

**IFRS terms to know**
- Carrying amount (*valeur nette comptable*): amount at which an asset is recognised after deducting accumulated depreciation and impairment losses (IAS 16 §6).
- Depreciation (PP&E, IAS 16) and amortisation (intangible assets, IAS 38): both translate *amortissement*. *Dépréciation* is an impairment loss (IAS 36).
- Recoverable amount: the higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use (IAS 36 §18).
- Fair value: price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants (IFRS 13 §9).
- Other comprehensive income (OCI, *autres éléments du résultat global*), non-controlling interests (*intérêts minoritaires / participations ne donnant pas le contrôle*), retained earnings (*réserves et report à nouveau*).

**UK vs US usage** (UK first, then US; IFRS wording in brackets)
- Turnover / sales (revenue) – *chiffre d'affaires*
- Stock / inventory (inventories) – *stocks*
- Debtors / accounts receivable (trade receivables) – *créances clients*
- Creditors / accounts payable (trade payables) – *dettes fournisseurs*
- Shares / stock (shares) – *actions* ; share premium / additional paid-in capital – *prime d'émission*
- Profit and loss account / income statement – *compte de résultat* ; gearing / leverage – *endettement*

## Key points
- "Stock" means inventories in traditional UK accounts but shares in US English ("common stock").
- US spelling is "amortization"; IFRS (British spelling) uses "amortisation".
- Recoverable amount takes the higher of two values, not the lower: an asset is impaired when its carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount.
- IFRS adopted in the EU use British spelling and wording ("inventories", "trade receivables").
