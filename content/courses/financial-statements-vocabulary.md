# Financial statements vocabulary: balance sheet, income statement, cash flow statement

**References:** IFRS 18 §10, §47, §69, §78-83, §96-103 and §117-118 ; IAS 7 §6, §10-17, §33A and §34A as amended by IFRS 18 (règlement UE 2023/1803 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/338) ; IFRS 16 §50

**Key issue:** reading an English set of accounts (UK, US or IFRS) requires naming each statement and each line item correctly; the exam tests the vocabulary through extracts of annual reports and short translations, never through IFRS technicalities for their own sake.

**Complete set of financial statements (IFRS 18 §10, formerly IAS 1 §10):** statement of financial position (balance sheet, *bilan*), statement(s) of financial performance: statement of profit or loss (income statement, *compte de résultat*) and statement presenting comprehensive income, statement of changes in equity (*tableau de variation des capitaux propres*), statement of cash flows (*tableau des flux de trésorerie*) and notes (*annexe*). IFRS 18, issued by the IASB in 2024 and endorsed by the EU (Regulation (EU) 2026/338 of 13 February 2026), replaces IAS 1 for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

**Balance sheet:** assets (*actif*) = equity (*capitaux propres*) + liabilities (*dettes et provisions*).
- Current vs non-current (IFRS 18 §99-102): an asset is current if it is expected to be realised, sold or consumed within the normal operating cycle or within twelve months after the reporting period, or if it is cash; a liability is current if it is due within twelve months or if the entity has no right, at the end of the reporting period, to defer settlement for at least twelve months (§101). Everything else is non-current.
- Typical items: property, plant and equipment (PP&E), intangible assets, inventories, trade receivables, cash and cash equivalents; share capital, share premium, retained earnings; borrowings, trade payables, accruals, provisions.

**Income statement:** under IFRS 18, income and expenses are classified in five categories (operating, investing, financing, income taxes, discontinued operations, §47), with two required subtotals before profit for the year: operating profit (*résultat d'exploitation*) and profit before financing and income taxes (§69). Typical lines: revenue (*chiffre d'affaires*), cost of sales, gross profit (*marge brute*), distribution costs, administrative expenses, operating profit, share of profit of associates and joint ventures (investing), finance costs (financing), income tax expense, profit for the year (*résultat net*). Operating expenses are presented by nature (raw materials, employee benefits, depreciation) or by function (cost of sales, distribution, administration); a company using functions discloses depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment losses and inventory write-downs in a single note (§78-83).

**Cash flow statement (IAS 7 §10):** operating activities (*exploitation*: cash from customers, to suppliers and employees, tax paid), investing activities (*investissement*: purchase or disposal of PP&E, acquisitions of subsidiaries) and financing activities (*financement*: share issues, borrowings and repayments, principal portion of lease payments under IFRS 16 §50). From 2027, a company whose main business is neither investing nor providing finance to customers classifies interest and dividends paid in financing activities and interest and dividends received in investing activities (IAS 7 §33A and §34A); before, a consistent choice was allowed.

**Key formulas:** gross profit = revenue − cost of sales ; EBITDA = operating profit + depreciation + amortisation ; current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities ; quick ratio = (current assets − inventories) ÷ current liabilities.

## Example
Extract from the balance sheet of Dunmore Ltd at 31 December 2025 (€k): PP&E 1,100; inventories 150; trade receivables 210; cash and cash equivalents 90; equity 780; long-term borrowings 370; trade payables 220; short-term borrowings 80. Income statement: revenue 2,400; cost of sales 1,500.
- Current assets = 150 + 210 + 90 = 450 ; current liabilities = 220 + 80 = 300 (the long-term borrowings are non-current).
- Current ratio = 450 ÷ 300 = 1.5 ; quick ratio = (450 − 150) ÷ 300 = 1.0.
- Gross profit = 2,400 − 1,500 = 900, i.e. a gross margin of 37.5% of revenue.

## Common mistakes
- Treating the proceeds from the disposal of a building as an operating cash flow: it is an investing activity; only cash from customers and to suppliers belongs to operating activities.
- Classifying the principal repaid on a lease liability as operating: under IFRS 16 §50 it is a financing cash flow (the interest portion follows the rules for interest paid: financing activities from 2027, IAS 7 §34A).
- Translating *résultat net* by "net result": in a set of accounts the word is "profit for the year" (UK) or "net income" (US).
- Mixing up accruals and prepayments: accruals (*charges à payer*) are current liabilities, prepayments (*charges constatées d'avance*) are current assets.

## Key points
- Four primary statements plus the notes in a complete set (IFRS 18 §10, which replaces IAS 1 from 2027); the balance sheet is called the "statement of financial position" in IFRS wording.
- EBITDA is not defined by IFRS: an adjusted EBITDA used in public communications is a management-defined performance measure, reconciled in a single note to the most comparable IFRS subtotal (IFRS 18 §117-123).
- Dividends paid are financing cash flows (IAS 7 §33A, from 2027; before, financing or operating), never investing cash flows.
- "Profit" or "income" translates *résultat*; never "result".
