# Financial statements vocabulary: balance sheet, income statement, cash flow statement

**References:** IAS 1 §10, §54, §66-76, §82 and §99-105 ; IAS 7 §6, §10-17 and §34 ; IFRS 16 §50 (règlement UE 2023/1803)

**Key issue:** reading an English set of accounts (UK, US or IFRS) requires naming each statement and each line item correctly; the exam tests the vocabulary through extracts of annual reports and short translations, never through IFRS technicalities for their own sake.

**Complete set of financial statements (IAS 1 §10):** statement of financial position (balance sheet, *bilan*), statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income (income statement, *compte de résultat*), statement of changes in equity (*tableau de variation des capitaux propres*), statement of cash flows (*tableau des flux de trésorerie*) and notes (*annexe*). IFRS 18, issued by the IASB in 2024 and endorsed by the EU (Regulation (EU) 2026/338 of 13 February 2026), replaces IAS 1 for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

**Balance sheet:** assets (*actif*) = equity (*capitaux propres*) + liabilities (*dettes et provisions*).
- Current vs non-current (IAS 1 §66-76): an asset is current if it is expected to be realised, sold or consumed within the normal operating cycle or within twelve months after the reporting period, or if it is cash; a liability is current if it is due within twelve months or if the entity has no unconditional right to defer settlement. Everything else is non-current.
- Typical items: property, plant and equipment (PP&E), intangible assets, inventories, trade receivables, cash and cash equivalents; share capital, share premium, retained earnings; borrowings, trade payables, accruals, provisions.

**Income statement:** revenue (*chiffre d'affaires*), cost of sales, gross profit (*marge brute*), distribution costs, administrative expenses, operating profit, finance costs, profit before tax, income tax expense, profit for the year (*résultat net*). Expenses are analysed by nature (raw materials, staff costs, depreciation) or by function (cost of sales, distribution, administration); a company presenting by function must disclose depreciation and staff costs in the notes (IAS 1 §99-105).

**Cash flow statement (IAS 7 §10):** operating activities (*exploitation*: cash from customers, to suppliers and employees, tax paid), investing activities (*investissement*: purchase or disposal of PP&E, acquisitions of subsidiaries) and financing activities (*financement*: share issues, borrowings and repayments, principal portion of lease payments under IFRS 16 §50). Interest and dividends paid may be shown in operating or financing activities, consistently from one year to the next (IAS 7 §31-34).

**Key formulas:** gross profit = revenue − cost of sales ; EBITDA = operating profit + depreciation + amortisation ; current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities ; quick ratio = (current assets − inventories) ÷ current liabilities.

## Example
Extract from the balance sheet of Dunmore Ltd at 31 December 2025 (€k): PP&E 1,100; inventories 150; trade receivables 210; cash and cash equivalents 90; equity 780; long-term borrowings 370; trade payables 220; short-term borrowings 80. Income statement: revenue 2,400; cost of sales 1,500.
- Current assets = 150 + 210 + 90 = 450 ; current liabilities = 220 + 80 = 300 (the long-term borrowings are non-current).
- Current ratio = 450 ÷ 300 = 1.5 ; quick ratio = (450 − 150) ÷ 300 = 1.0.
- Gross profit = 2,400 − 1,500 = 900, i.e. a gross margin of 37.5% of revenue.

## Common mistakes
- Treating the proceeds from the disposal of a building as an operating cash flow: it is an investing activity; only cash from customers and to suppliers belongs to operating activities.
- Classifying the principal repaid on a lease liability as operating: under IFRS 16 §50 it is a financing cash flow (the interest follows the company's policy for interest paid).
- Translating *résultat net* by "net result": in a set of accounts the word is "profit for the year" (UK) or "net income" (US).
- Mixing up accruals and prepayments: accruals (*charges à payer*) are current liabilities, prepayments (*charges constatées d'avance*) are current assets.

## Key points
- Five statements in a complete set under IAS 1; the balance sheet is called the "statement of financial position" in IFRS wording.
- EBITDA is not defined by IFRS: it is an alternative performance measure, to be reconciled with an IFRS line item.
- Dividends paid may be shown in operating or financing activities (IAS 7 §34), never in investing activities.
- "Profit" or "income" translates *résultat*; never "result".
