# Audit and assurance vocabulary

**References:** ISA 200, 320, 450, 501, 505, 570, 580, 701, 705 and 706 (IAASB) ; ISRE 2410 ; NEP 320, 700 and 701 (French standards based on the ISAs)

**Key issue:** the audit vocabulary of UE 4 has an exact English equivalent in the ISAs; the exam uses extracts of auditor's reports or engagement letters and asks you to identify the level of assurance, the type of opinion or the procedure described.

**Players and engagements**
- Statutory auditor (*commissaire aux comptes*), external vs internal auditor, audit committee (*comité d'audit*), those charged with governance (*gouvernance*), engagement letter (*lettre de mission*), engagement partner (*associé signataire*).
- Audit: **reasonable assurance** (*assurance raisonnable*) that the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and give a true and fair view (*image fidèle*). Review (e.g. interim financial statements under ISRE 2410): **limited assurance** (*assurance modérée* in NEP 2410, also *assurance limitée*), based mainly on inquiry and analytical procedures, expressed in a negative form ("nothing has come to our attention").

**Planning and evidence**
- Materiality (*seuil de signification*, ISA 320): in practice a percentage of a benchmark such as profit before tax (often 5%), revenue or total assets; performance materiality (*seuil de planification*) is set lower to allow for undetected misstatements; the "clearly trivial" threshold (*seuil des anomalies manifestement insignifiantes*) is far below both.
- Misstatement (*anomalie*), assertions (*assertions*: occurrence, completeness, accuracy, cut-off, classification, existence, rights and obligations, valuation), audit evidence (*éléments probants*), sampling (*sondages*), walkthrough, tests of controls, substantive procedures, analytical procedures.
- External confirmation or circularisation (ISA 505), attendance at physical inventory counting (*assistance à l'inventaire physique*, ISA 501), written representations (*lettre d'affirmation*, ISA 580), subsequent events (*événements postérieurs*).
- Misstatements other than clearly trivial are accumulated and communicated to management, which is asked to correct them; uncorrected misstatements are assessed individually and in aggregate against materiality (ISA 450).

**Auditor's report (ISA 700-706)**
- Unmodified opinion (*certification sans réserve*): the financial statements give a true and fair view (or "present fairly, in all material respects").
- Modified opinions (ISA 705): **qualified** ("except for", *certification avec réserve*: misstatement or lack of evidence material but **not pervasive**), **adverse** (*refus de certifier*: misstatements material and pervasive), **disclaimer of opinion** (*impossibilité de certifier*: unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence, possible effects material and pervasive).
- Emphasis of matter paragraph (*observation*, ISA 706), key audit matters (KAM, ISA 701; French *points clés de l'audit* in the *justification des appréciations*), material uncertainty related to going concern (ISA 570).

## Example
Lindell plc reports a profit before tax of €4,000k. The auditor sets materiality at 5% of profit before tax = €200k, performance materiality at 75% of materiality = €150k and the clearly trivial threshold at 5% of materiality = €10k. Three uncorrected misstatements remain at the end of the audit: 60, 70 and 90 (€k).
- Each one is below materiality, but the aggregate is 60 + 70 + 90 = €220k > €200k: the uncorrected misstatements are material in aggregate.
- Management refuses to adjust. The misstatements affect two balance sheet lines only, so they are not pervasive: the auditor expresses a **qualified** opinion ("except for the effects of the matters described in the Basis for qualified opinion section").

## Common mistakes
- Adding an emphasis of matter paragraph to deal with an unresolved material misstatement: an emphasis of matter draws attention to a matter correctly presented and never replaces a modification of the opinion.
- Expressing an adverse opinion for a material but not pervasive misstatement: material alone leads to a qualified opinion; adverse requires material **and** pervasive.
- Confusing disclaimer and adverse opinion: a disclaimer results from a lack of evidence, an adverse opinion from misstatements the auditor has identified.
- Believing that a review gives reasonable assurance: a review gives limited assurance only, with procedures far less extensive than an audit.

## Key points
- "Pervasive" is the key word that separates a qualified opinion from an adverse opinion or a disclaimer.
- Materiality > performance materiality > clearly trivial threshold; uncorrected misstatements are assessed in aggregate.
- Review ≠ audit: a review gives limited, not reasonable, assurance.
