# Corporate governance: boards, shareholders, executive pay

**References:** UK Corporate Governance Code (FRC) ; Afep-Medef Corporate Governance Code ; Directive (EU) 2017/828 (Shareholder Rights Directive II) ; Directive 2006/43/EC, art. 39, as amended by Directive 2014/56/EU (audit committees)

- **Agency theory**: managers (agents) may not act in the interest of shareholders (principals); governance mechanisms reduce this **principal-agent problem**.
- **Board structures**: unitary or one-tier board (board of directors, *conseil d'administration*) vs two-tier structure (supervisory board + management board, *conseil de surveillance et directoire*).
- **Directors**: executive directors manage the company; non-executive directors (NEDs) oversee and challenge; **independent directors** have no material relationship with the company. Splitting the roles of chair and CEO strengthens oversight; when combined, codes often recommend a lead independent director.
- **Board committees**: audit, remuneration (compensation), nomination. The **audit committee** monitors the financial (and sustainability) reporting process, the effectiveness of internal control and risk management, the statutory audit and the auditor's independence, and recommends the auditor's appointment. Under art. 39, a majority of members are independent and at least one member has competence in accounting and/or auditing.
- **Shareholders**: vote at the AGM (*assemblée générale*), often by proxy; **proxy advisers** issue voting recommendations; institutional investors follow stewardship codes.
- **Shareholder activism**: activist investors (often hedge funds) take a stake and push for change — share buybacks, higher dividends, spin-offs, board seats, CEO replacement — through engagement, public campaigns or a **proxy fight**.
- **Executive pay**: base salary, annual bonus, long-term incentive plan (LTIP, e.g. performance shares); **malus** (reduction before vesting) and **clawback** (recovery after payment). **Say on pay**: SRD II gives shareholders a vote on the remuneration policy and on the remuneration report.
- **"Comply or explain"**: listed companies following a code must apply its provisions or explain any departure.

## Key points
- Independent ≠ non-executive: a former executive can be non-executive without being independent.
- The audit committee oversees the audit; it does not prepare the accounts.
- Under SRD II, the policy vote may be binding or advisory depending on the member state.
