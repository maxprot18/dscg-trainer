# Corporate governance: boards, shareholders, executive pay

**References:** UK Corporate Governance Code (FRC, 2024) ; Afep-Medef Corporate Governance Code ; Directive (EU) 2017/828 (Shareholder Rights Directive II), art. 9a-9b ; Directive 2006/43/EC, art. 39, as amended by Directive 2014/56/EU (audit committees) ; G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance (2023)

**Key issue:** governance sections of annual reports and press coverage of AGM votes are typical UE 6 reading material; you must describe board structures, the audit committee's role and say-on-pay rules in precise English.

- **Agency theory**: managers (agents) may not act in the interest of shareholders (principals); governance mechanisms — board oversight, disclosure, incentive pay, shareholder votes — reduce this **principal-agent problem**. The OECD principles add the rights of stakeholders and transparency.
- **Board structures**: unitary or one-tier board (board of directors, *conseil d'administration*) vs two-tier structure (supervisory board + management board, *conseil de surveillance et directoire*). Employee representatives sit on the board in several countries (Germany, France above a size threshold).
- **Directors**: executive directors manage the company; non-executive directors (NEDs) oversee and constructively challenge management without running operations; **independent directors** have no material relationship with the company (codes exclude former executives for five years, major customers or suppliers, and often directors with more than twelve years of service). Splitting the roles of chair and CEO strengthens oversight; when combined, codes recommend a lead independent director.
- **Board committees**: audit, remuneration (compensation), nomination. The **audit committee** monitors the financial (and sustainability) reporting process, the effectiveness of internal control and risk management, the statutory audit and the auditor's independence, and recommends the auditor's appointment. Under art. 39, a majority of members are independent and at least one member has competence in accounting and/or auditing. It does not prepare the accounts and does not set executive pay.
- **Shareholders**: vote at the AGM (*assemblée générale*), often by proxy; **proxy advisers** (ISS, Glass Lewis) issue voting recommendations; institutional investors follow stewardship codes and engage with boards.
- **Shareholder activism**: activist investors (often hedge funds) take a stake and push for change — share buybacks, higher dividends, spin-offs, board seats, CEO replacement — through engagement, public campaigns or a **proxy fight**.
- **Executive pay**: base salary, annual bonus, long-term incentive plan (LTIP, e.g. performance shares vesting after three years); **malus** (reduction before vesting) and **clawback** (recovery after payment). **Say on pay**: SRD II gives shareholders a vote on the remuneration policy (ex ante) and on the remuneration report (ex post); in France both votes are binding, and variable pay for the year cannot be paid if the ex post vote fails.
- **"Comply or explain"**: listed companies following a code must apply its provisions or explain any departure in the governance report.

## Example
Vector plc, listed in London, has a board of ten: the chair, the CEO, the CFO, a former CFO who retired two years ago, a director representing a 15% shareholder and five other NEDs with no links to the company. The nomination committee asks how many directors are independent and whether the audit committee can be chaired by the former CFO.
Solution: the former CFO is non-executive but not independent (executive within the last five years); the shareholder representative is not independent either. Independent directors = **5 out of 10**, which meets the Code's "at least half, excluding the chair" guideline (5 of 9). The audit committee must be made up of independent NEDs, at least one of whom has recent and relevant financial experience: the former CFO cannot sit on it, let alone chair it; one of the five independent NEDs with a finance background should.

## Common mistakes
- Describing the NED's role as running a division or auditing the accounts: NEDs oversee and challenge; the auditor audits.
- Listing "preparing the financial statements" or "setting executive pay" among audit committee tasks: those belong to management and to the remuneration committee.
- Assuming the SRD II say-on-pay vote is always binding: the directive lets member states make the policy vote advisory.
- Equating "non-executive" with "independent".

## Key points
- Independent ≠ non-executive: a former executive can be non-executive without being independent.
- The audit committee oversees the audit; it does not prepare the accounts.
- Under SRD II, the policy vote may be binding or advisory depending on the member state.
