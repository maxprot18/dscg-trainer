# Monetary and fiscal policy

**References:** TFEU art. 127(1) (ECB primary objective: price stability) ; TFEU art. 126 and Protocol No 12 (excessive deficit procedure, Stability and Growth Pact) ; Federal Reserve Act (dual mandate)

- **Central bank mandates**: the ECB's primary objective is price stability (medium-term inflation target of 2%, symmetric, since its 2021 strategy review); the US Federal Reserve has a **dual mandate** (maximum employment and stable prices); the Bank of England targets 2% CPI inflation.
- **Tools**: policy (key) interest rates — for the ECB, the deposit facility rate, the main refinancing operations rate and the marginal lending facility rate; open market operations; **forward guidance**; **quantitative easing** (QE: large-scale asset purchases that expand the central bank balance sheet) and its reverse, **quantitative tightening** (QT).
- **Transmission**: a rate hike raises borrowing costs, reduces credit demand and asset prices, tends to strengthen the currency, and so cools demand and inflation — with long and variable lags.
- **Stance**: tightening / hawkish (fighting inflation) vs easing / dovish (supporting activity).
- **Fiscal policy**: use of government spending and taxation. The **budget deficit** is a flow (one year); **public debt** is a stock (accumulated deficits). EU reference values: 3% of GDP for the deficit, 60% of GDP for debt.
- **Automatic stabilisers** (tax receipts, unemployment benefits) cushion the cycle without new decisions; discretionary policy can be expansionary (stimulus) or contractionary (austerity). **Crowding out**: public borrowing may push up rates and displace private investment.
- **Policy mix**: combination of monetary and fiscal stances.

**Key formulas:** Fisher: (1 + i) = (1 + r)(1 + π), so r = (1 + i) ÷ (1 + π) − 1 ≈ i − π ; Taylor rule: i = r* + π + 0.5 (π − π*) + 0.5 × output gap

## Key points
- A negative real interest rate (nominal rate below inflation) is still accommodative, even after several hikes.
- Deficit (flow) ≠ debt (stock): a country can cut its deficit while its debt ratio keeps rising.
- In the euro area, monetary policy is single but fiscal policy remains national, within EU fiscal rules.
