# Monetary and fiscal policy

**References:** TFEU art. 127(1) (ECB primary objective: price stability) ; TFEU art. 126 and Protocol No 12 (excessive deficit procedure, 3% and 60% reference values) ; Regulation (EU) 2024/1263 (reformed Stability and Growth Pact) ; Federal Reserve Act, section 2A (dual mandate)

**Key issue:** central bank decisions and government budgets are the most frequent subject of the economic texts given in UE 6; you must explain a rate decision, its transmission to the economy, and distinguish deficit from debt.

- **Central bank mandates**: the ECB's primary objective is price stability (2% inflation target over the medium term, symmetric, since its 2021 strategy review, confirmed by the 2025 strategy assessment); the US Federal Reserve has a **dual mandate** (maximum employment and stable prices); the Bank of England targets 2% CPI inflation. Central banks are independent from governments so that they can raise rates even when unpopular.
- **Tools**: policy (key) interest rates — for the ECB, the deposit facility rate (the main signal today), the main refinancing operations rate and the marginal lending facility rate; open market operations; **forward guidance** (communication on future policy); **quantitative easing** (QE: large-scale asset purchases that expand the central bank balance sheet and push long-term yields down) and its reverse, **quantitative tightening** (QT).
- **Transmission**: a rate hike raises bank lending rates, slows credit demand, lowers asset prices (existing bond prices fall), tends to strengthen the currency (imports become cheaper), and so cools demand and inflation — with long and variable lags, usually estimated at 12 to 24 months.
- **Stance**: tightening / hawkish (fighting inflation) vs easing / dovish (supporting activity). A rate is accommodative as long as it is below the neutral rate, or negative in real terms.
- **Fiscal policy**: use of government spending and taxation. The **budget deficit** is a flow (one year); **public debt** is a stock (accumulated deficits). EU reference values: 3% of GDP for the deficit, 60% of GDP for debt; since 2024 each member state follows a multi-year net expenditure path.
- **Automatic stabilisers** (tax receipts fall and unemployment benefits rise in a downturn) cushion the cycle without new decisions; discretionary policy can be expansionary (stimulus) or contractionary (austerity). **Crowding out**: public borrowing may push up rates and displace private investment.
- **Policy mix**: combination of monetary and fiscal stances; a loose fiscal policy with a tight monetary policy pulls in opposite directions.

**Key formulas:** Fisher: (1 + i) = (1 + r)(1 + π), so r = (1 + i) ÷ (1 + π) − 1 ≈ i − π ; Taylor rule: i = r* + π + 0.5 (π − π*) + 0.5 × output gap ; debt ratio next year = (debt + deficit) ÷ (GDP × (1 + nominal growth))

## Example
A firm borrows at a nominal rate of 4.5% while expected inflation is 3.0%: exact real rate = 1.045 ÷ 1.03 − 1 = **1.46%** (approximation: 1.5%). Taylor rule with r* = 1%, inflation 3%, target 2%, output gap −1%: i = 1 + 3 + 0.5 × 1 + 0.5 × (−1) = **4.0%**.
Fiscal side: debt 110% of GDP, deficit 4% of GDP, nominal GDP growth 3%. Debt ratio next year = (110 + 4) ÷ 1.03 = **110.7%**: the ratio keeps rising although the deficit is "only" 4%; cutting the deficit to 2% gives (110 + 2) ÷ 1.03 = 108.7%.

## Common mistakes
- Expecting a rate hike to make existing fixed-rate bond prices rise: higher yields mean lower prices.
- Expecting the currency to depreciate after a hike: higher rates attract capital and tend to strengthen it.
- Believing inflation reacts within the month: transmission takes several quarters.
- Treating a deficit cut as a debt cut: the debt stock still grows as long as there is a deficit.

## Key points
- A negative real interest rate (nominal rate below inflation) is still accommodative, even after several hikes.
- Deficit (flow) ≠ debt (stock): a country can cut its deficit while its debt ratio keeps rising.
- In the euro area, monetary policy is single but fiscal policy remains national, within EU fiscal rules.
